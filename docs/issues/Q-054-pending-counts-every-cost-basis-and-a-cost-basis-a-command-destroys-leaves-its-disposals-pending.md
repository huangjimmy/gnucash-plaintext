# Q-054 — `$pending$` counts every cost basis, on its own side and never below nothing, and a command that destroys a cost basis leaves what drew on it pending

## What was reported

A user's application imported a sale of US dollars an invoice had collected with `cost_basis_split_guid: $pending$`, in a book whose only US dollar cost basis was that invoice's. It was refused: "on 2026-09-01 the book had no cost basis for the USD it held, so there is no cost basis to choose". The same sale stating the invoice's guid was accepted (`2026-09-26-a-collected-invoices-cost-basis-is-refused-for-pending-but-accepted-by-guid.txt`, in the application's evidence). The same refusal met a repayment of US dollars owed on a card that had paid a bill, whose only cost basis was the bill's.

The author then set out what every cost basis is, and what may consume it:

- A cost basis is a split, and belongs to no account. Its side is what its split makes grow. On an asset account, a split making the asset grow opens an asset cost basis, and one taking it below zero, where it acts as a liability, a liability cost basis. A liability is the mirror: growing opens a liability cost basis, and going below zero, where it acts as an asset, an asset cost basis.
- Every cost basis is consumable. The only restrictions are that a disposal of what the book holds consumes an asset cost basis and one of what it owes a liability cost basis, and that nothing consumes a cost basis below nothing.
- A split crossing zero does both: the part up to zero consumes the cost basis the account held, and the part past zero opens one on the other side, at the rate the currency was acquired at. The author's case: an asset account at −500 holds a liability cost basis of 500; 1,000 of income arriving into it takes it to 500, and the liability cost basis is consumed and an asset cost basis of 500 opened. With rates, which are this doc's and not the author's: a bank at −500.00 USD owed at 1.30, into which 1,000.00 USD bought at 1.20 is deposited, consumes the 500.00 owed and realizes 500.00 × (1.30 − 1.20) = 50.00, and opens an asset cost basis of 500.00 at 1.20.
- An overpayment makes two new cost bases. 200.00 USD paid into a US dollar bank for a 100.00 USD invoice collects the invoice, and the other 100.00 does not belong to the company yet: past zero on the receivable it is a liability cost basis, and on the bank an asset cost basis, each at the payment's own rate.
- A command that destroys a cost basis is not refused for what drew on it: the user is not made to choose another cost basis before it can run. What drew on it is made pending.

## Why it happened

- `a_cost_basis_is_kept_for` and `_a_cost_basis_opened_by`, which `$pending$` and a disposal stating no guid both ask, passed over every cost basis on a receivable or a payable. An invoice's and a bill's were never counted.
- `$pending$` required the split's whole amount to be its disposal, so a split crossing zero was refused however little it disposed of, and nothing compared a pending disposal with what its side's cost bases had left: 3,000.00 USD pending against 2,720.00 held was imported.
- A customer's credit on the receivable was read as the cost of the dollars the overpayment brought into the bank, so the bank's split opened no cost basis, and a bank sale was allowed to draw on the credit.
- The balance sheet counted a receivable or a payable on its type's side whatever its balance, so a receivable below zero was subtracted from the held side.
- `unpost-invoices`, `unpost-bills` and an import rebuilding a record refused while anything drew on the record's cost basis.

## What changes

- Every cost basis counts for `$pending$` and for a disposal stating no guid, whatever account it is on. `the_side_of_a_cost_basis` reads its side, and every place that sorts cost bases by side asks it; `fx-balances` shows it in a SIDE column, and totals each side apart: `Total USD cost basis balance: 200.00 USD held, 100.00 USD owed`.
- A pending disposal is the part its split disposes of, subtracted at the rate its transaction records for it, and is refused where it and the other pending disposals of its side would take more than that side's cost bases have left.
- A disposal stating a guid draws only on a cost basis of its own side, a receivable's and a payable's included.
- An overpayment into a foreign bank opens a liability cost basis on the credit and an asset cost basis on the bank's split, for what arrived past what was collected (`brought_in_by`), each at the rates file's rate for the payment's date. This holds for a payment GnuCash writes, for a bank transaction the import divides by `txn_guid:`, and for one the file states already divided with `prepayment:`.
- A bill overpaid out of a US dollar bank holding none is the mirror. The bank goes from 0 to −200.00 USD. The first 100.00 is the bill paid, and the bill's cost basis stands for it. The other 100.00 is the vendor's: an asset cost basis on the payable, and a liability cost basis of 100.00 on the bank's split.
- Spending a customer's credit on an invoice consumes two cost bases: the credit's, owed, and the invoice's, held. The split the credit puts in the invoice's lot states the invoice's posting split as its cost basis, and the invoice's balance falls by it. No dollar arrives in a bank, so the credit's split is not counted as collected beside the bank's split, and the bank keeps the cost basis the overpayment opened.
- Unposting an invoice a credit was spent on hands the credit back as the customer's again: its guid comes off the split, and its liability cost basis opens at its amount. A settlement split on a receivable or a payable drawing on the record is never made pending, since the unpost leaves it disposing of nothing.
- A linked payment converting a record's currency, a USD invoice collected into a Canadian dollar bank by `txn_guid:`, is answered as the payment GnuCash writes is. The settlement draws the record's cost basis down and is restated at the record's cost, and the difference goes on the payment block's `$residual$` line. A block with a difference and no such line is refused. Linked without this, the invoice's cost basis stood for dollars the book no longer held, and the balance sheet did not balance by the 3.00 between them. A transaction whose settling split already states the record's posting split as its cost basis, at the record's cost with the difference in the transaction itself, is left as the file wrote it. That is how an export writes it.
- The payment GnuCash writes for a bill overpaid out of a US dollar bank holding none records what the bank's split brought in past zero, as the import does for a transaction a file states, so the bank's liability cost basis opens either way, and a book rebuilt from its export matches it.
- An unpost removes the guid from a split only where the split is in the same lot as the cost basis it draws on, which is the record's own settlement. A bill paid by a transaction of its own, in no lot, is a disposal like any other and is made pending.
- `find-orphan-payments` passes over the split that took a settlement's `$residual$` when it states the account the money came through.
- A credit on a receivable in no lot opens a liability cost basis for what it takes the account past zero, and a debit on a payable the mirror. A customer's overpayment in no lot is then two cost bases, one on each side, as it is in a lot of the customer's.
- A refund paying back an owner's credit, a split in a lot of the owner's moving it toward zero, consumes the credit's cost basis and states it with `cost_basis_split_guid:` or `$pending$`. Stating none, it is refused, as any disposal stating none is. Imported without one, the credit's cost basis stood for currency nobody was owed any more.
- A bill overpaid out of a US dollar bank that holds dollars is refused, since the payment GnuCash writes spends a cost basis and cannot say which. The settlement is written as a transaction of its own, stating `cost_basis_split_guid:` on the bank line, and attached with `txn_guid:`.
- The balance sheet reads a receivable or a payable by its balance, as it reads a bank: below zero it is on the owed side.
- `unpost-invoices`, `unpost-bills` and an import that unposts a record write `cost_basis_split_guid: $pending$` on each disposal that drew on the record's cost basis, and list them. The command goes through whatever else the book keeps: where the record's cost basis was the only one on its side, the disposals made pending draw on nothing the book keeps yet, and `fx-balances --verify-costs` reports each until an edit states the cost basis it draws on.
- `delete-transactions` of a transaction holding a cost basis makes each disposal that drew on it pending, rather than being refused, and lists them. A disposal in a transaction the same command deletes, a fee drawing on its own transaction's arrival or a sale deleted with its purchase, is deleted too, and is neither made pending nor listed. `unapply-payment` reopens whole a cost basis whose split the restatement leaves bringing in another amount, and makes what drew on it pending: an overpaid invoice's payment unapplied to a Canadian dollar account leaves the bank's split bringing in all 200.00, and it opens at 200.00. The same holds for a split the restatement makes a cost basis again with disposals still drawing on it: a vendor claim 80.00 of which was sold, spent on a bill and moved onto a US dollar bank by the unapply, opens at 100.00 and the sale is made pending.
- None of these commands works out again what a disposal drew. The user states it, by editing the file and importing it again. The tests act as that user: each knows every state of its book, and each goes one path to a book `--verify-integrity` passes.
- An edit clearing a split's `$pending$` with `cost_basis_split_guid: $None$` reads the split again, as a new split is read. A user who recorded a customer's payment of 100.00 USD ahead of time, sold 40.00 of it, and then deleted the payment when it did not come, clears the sale's pick: the sale takes the bank from 0 to −40.00, and opens a liability cost basis of 40.00 at the 1.40 it records. No command was added for it; the user does it with `delete-transactions` and an edit.
- `fx-balances` and `--verify-integrity` read a receivable or a payable lot by lot, as the balance sheet does: an invoice not collected is held beside a customer's credit owed back, where the account's balance of 0.00 would say it holds nothing and owes nothing.
- A book written before these rules keeps the cost bases it has. An overpayment it holds into a US dollar bank has one on the credit and none on the bank's split. Its export imported into a new book opens the bank's, since the payment block's `prepayment:` declares the overpayment and the rates file prices it; the credit arriving already in its lot through `lot_owner:` no longer stops that.
- The disposals pending on a side are read from the pick index, which files `$pending$` under its own key, so the balance sheet and `--verify-costs` walk no account per pending disposal. The import still refuses a pending disposal its side cannot cover, counting only cost bases open on its date. `--verify-costs` reports a side whose pending disposals together take more than its cost bases have left once, and the balance sheet still subtracts each of them. A disposal stating a guid takes no more than its account holds, so it cannot take what a pending one needs.
- Whether an edit clears a split's `$pending$` is read from that split's own block, by its `guid:` or its account: a block stating no `cost_basis_split_guid:` says nothing about it.
- `fx-balances --verify-costs` asks a disposal the book holds whether it draws on a cost basis of its own side, as the import asks a disposal in a file. A book an earlier release wrote may hold a sale of a bank's dollars drawing on a customer's credit; its export states the credit's guid on the sale, which the import refuses.
- A repayment of dollars owed may draw on a customer's credit, a cost basis of dollars owed. It is no invoice waiting to be collected, so it is not asked whether the invoice was collected. The credit spent in part afterwards, the repayment is pointed at the split holding the rest; spent whole, the export writes the repayment stating none.
- An import that unposts a record says on its output which disposals it made pending, once the file's change to the record has been accepted, as `unpost-invoices` lists them.
- A book keeping no cost bases (Q-049) links a payment converting a record's currency without restating it, and the block needs no `$residual$` line: there is no cost to restate the settlement at.
- `$None$`, unquoted, removes a custom key: on a transaction, a split, an account, a customer, a vendor, an invoice, a bill or the company block, and `cost_basis_split_guid:` on a split, which removes its `$pending$` or its guid.
- A field GnuCash keeps itself cannot be removed, so `$None$` on one is read as `#None`. Every field is then set as the block states it and read back, and where GnuCash keeps something other than what was stated, the import says so. What GnuCash keeps was measured after a save and a reload, on 5.10 and 3.4 (`tests/research/what_a_gnucash_field_reads_before_it_is_set_probe.py`): most text fields' setters ignore `None` and keep the value they had; an account's code and description, and an invoice's or a bill's billing id, store `""`; a transaction's doc link stores `None` only when set to `""`; an account's notes and colour store `None`. So a doc link stated `#None` or `$None$` is handed to GnuCash as `""`, and is `#None` afterwards, as stated.
- A split's `memo:` and a transaction's `notes:` cannot be set to `#None` either: their setters ignore a null. So `#None` and `$None$` on them set them to `""`, and the import warns that the field cannot be removed or set to #None, and that an empty field is not written in the export, which does not mean it was removed.
- `#None` is a value. A custom key is stored as JSON in one slot, and JSON holds a null, which another tool may write and which the export writes as `#None`. `#None` sets a custom key to null, on every block, the company block included.
- A book may keep an income or expense account in another currency, and its cost bases are then not expected to be correct: no cost basis records what the account's amounts cost, so whether such a book balances is not something any check can vouch for. `--verify-integrity` lists the accounts as a finding and exits 1. `fx-balances --verify-costs` prints them as a warning. The balance sheet and the income statement open with it. Each says to keep the account in the book's own currency or to turn cost bases off with `cost_bases: "off"` in the company block. A test on such a book asserts the warning, and never that its cost bases are correct.

## Cases the tests cover

Scenario tests, in `tests/scenario/`:

- `test_a_custom_key_on_a_bank_draft_is_set_changed_and_removed.py`: the author's case. A note kept as a custom key on a bank draft is set, corrected, joined by a second key, stated `"#None"` and `#None` without either removing it, and removed with `$None$`. Each step's import and export are fixtures, `tests/fixtures/a_bank_draft_*.txt`, and no step prints a warning.
- `test_a_gnucash_field_stated_none_keeps_what_gnucash_keeps_and_says_so.py`: the author's case of GnuCash's own fields on the same draft. The transaction's `notes:` and the split's `memo:` stated `$None$`, and then `#None`, are each set to `""` with a warning each, while `alert:` beside them is set; the split's `action:` stated `#None` keeps what GnuCash keeps, with the read-back's warning. Each step's import and export are fixtures, `tests/fixtures/a_draft_s_fields_*.txt`, and each step's warnings are stated, none where none is printed.

- `test_pending_counts_the_cost_basis_an_invoice_or_a_bill_opened.py`: the reported case, and its mirror on a bill.
- `test_every_cost_basis_is_consumable_on_its_own_side_and_never_below_nothing.py`: the invoice's and the bill's cost bases, a bank below zero, a card in credit, and a bank and a card crossing zero, each consumed by guid and pending, refused from the other side and past what it has left.
- `test_a_split_crossing_zero_consumes_one_cost_basis_and_opens_another.py`: the bank at −500.00 owing at 1.30 and the card 500.00 in credit, each taken across zero at 1.20, stating the guid and pending it; the export rebuilding each; an edit stating the guid making the pending book the stated one.

Integration tests:

- `test_unposting_a_record_makes_what_drew_on_its_cost_basis_pending.py`: an invoice and a bill unposted with a disposal on each one's cost basis, and the user then stating another cost basis on each.
- `test_a_sale_of_a_payment_that_never_came_becomes_a_borrowing.py`: the payment deleted, the sale made pending, and the user clearing its pick, after which it opens a liability cost basis.
- `test_deleting_a_cost_basis_makes_what_drew_on_it_pending.py`: a purchase deleted, the sale that drew on it made pending, and the user stating the borrowing's cost basis on it.

- `test_the_credit_an_overpaying_wire_leaves_is_counted_once_however_it_is_divided.py`: an invoice and a bill overpaid by a wire divided in the file, from the Canadian dollar bank stating the record's cost basis and the 3.00 difference, and into or out of the US dollar bank, each ending with `--verify-integrity` passing.
- `test_taking_a_wrong_link_off_restores_the_basis.py`: a vendor claim part sold, spent on a bill and moved onto a US dollar bank by `unapply-payment`; the sale made pending and the user stating the claim's cost basis on it again.
- `test_unlinking_a_linked_transaction.py`: a USD invoice linked to a Canadian dollar deposit by `txn_guid:`, its payment block stating the `$residual$` line, and the link removed with `unlink`.
- `test_metadata_written_by_another_tool.py`: a transaction another tool left holding `{"reviewed": null}`, its export edited and imported again keeping the null, and `reviewed: $None$` removing it. `test_company_custom_book_keys.py`: `#None` kept and `$None$` removing a company key. `test_none_on_a_split_s_action_and_memo.py`: `#None` and `$None$` on a memo set it to `""` with a warning, and `action: #None` keeps the action GnuCash has, with the read-back's warning.
- `test_an_overpaid_invoices_dollars_are_sold_against_a_cost_basis_of_their_side.py` also holds: a sale drawing on the credit written into the book, reported by `--verify-costs`; a card repayment drawing on the credit, then the credit spent in part and whole; the book checked on 2026-02-01, before the payment; and 10.00 USD pending out of the bank in a transaction stating no Canadian figure, refused. `test_unposting_a_record_makes_what_drew_on_its_cost_basis_pending.py`: an import with `posted: none` unposting the invoice under a sale, and saying so. `test_unlinking_a_linked_transaction.py`: a book keeping no cost bases linking the converting payment with no `$residual$` line.
- `test_foreign_currency_cost_basis.py`: a refund of a customer's credit stating the credit's cost basis, and one stating none refused.
- `test_deleting_a_cost_basis_makes_what_drew_on_it_pending.py`: a sale deleted with its purchase, and a fee drawing on its own transaction's arrival, neither listed as pending.

In every one the test is the user: it states each cost basis a pending disposal draws on, and gnucash-plaintext chooses none.

## Known, not yet done


- **`unapply-payment` onto the receivable itself.** `test_taking_a_payment_off_onto_its_receivable_leaves_the_owners_credit.py` takes an overpaid invoice's payment off onto its own receivable, and the book then holds two liability cost bases for one credit. Not yet worked through.
- **A collection in no lot.** An invoice's posting and the payment collecting it, both in no lot, are not read as a collection. The bank's split opens an asset cost basis for the 100.00 USD it received, and the posting keeps its own 100.00: the cost bases hold 200.00 against the 100.00 the bank holds. A credit in no lot past what the account held is read as owed back (`_past_what_the_account_held`); the collection beside it is not yet read as one. Linking the payment afterwards does not repair it. Measured with a 200.00 USD wire into `Assets:Bank:USD`, entered in CAD (278.00 CAD: the settling 100.00 at the invoice's 1.40, the customer's 100.00 at 1.38), imported ahead of INV-USD-WIRE in the same file and linked by its payment block with `txn_split_guid:` and `prepayment: 100`. `fx-balances --verify-costs` then lists the bank's cost basis at 200.00 USD against the 100.00 it brought in past the invoice, and the settling split keeps the 100.00 USD liability cost basis it opened when it took the receivable below zero, though it is the invoice's settlement.
- **The balance sheet after an unpost.** The unpost leaves the payment that collected the invoice orphaned, dollars held that no cost basis stands for, until the invoice is posted again and the payment linked to it. The tests reach a consistent book that way, by importing the invoice again.
- **A payment in no lot paying down a record.** A split on a receivable or a payable that is in no lot and moves it toward zero, such as a bill paid by a transaction that applies to no bill, consumes a cost basis, and is not yet refused when it states none. A refund in a lot of the owner's is refused (above).
- **A difference between a credit's cost and the invoice it is spent on.** Spending the credit consumes both cost bases, and where they were taken on at different rates a difference is realized. The tests spend credits at the rate the invoice was posted at, where there is none. Where the difference goes is not yet written.
