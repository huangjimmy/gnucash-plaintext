## Problem

There is no multi-currency support.

A business that invoices a US customer, is billed by a US vendor, or holds a USD account cannot keep its books with this tool: a foreign-currency invoice or bill is written wrong, a payment that converts is booked at 1:1, revenue cannot be recognized in the currency the business reports in, and nothing records what the received currency cost or what converting it realized.

None of it fails loudly. The import exits 0, prints a success summary, and `validate` finds nothing — so the books look right and are wrong.

## Scope

Nothing in the design is specific to USD and CAD — a basis balance at a cost, identified by the split guid, works the same in whatever currency the book reports in. But the tests and the documentation cover **USD against a CAD base book only**. A book holding a third currency is not tested and is not claimed to work.

## USD/CAD support for CAD base book

Invoice: when posted, we have A/R +100 USD, INCOME -140 CAD, so we know we have 100 balance of USD at cost basis 1.4 CAD/USD.
If the invoice is paid, the USD balance 100 USD at 1.4 cost basis is realized.
If the invoice is written off, what happens to a previous conversion from USD to CAD that picked this 100 USD balance cost? We don't modify the USD/CAD conversion tx; instead we treat this as a separate income adjustment at the written-off date.

No matter what, the A/R +100 USD split identifies and tracks available USD balance up to 100 USD at cost 1.4 CAD/USD.

Bill: we do the mirror operation of Invoice.

Borrowing of USD: A/P -135 CAD, Bank +100 USD
Purchase of USD: Bank -135 CAD, Bank +100 USD
Either case, the Bank +100 USD split identifies and tracks available USD balance up to 100 USD at cost 1.35 CAD/USD.

Upon selling USD for CAD, we will have

```
USD Account -100 USD
CAD Account +139 CAD
FX Gain -1 CAD          ; if we pick the cost 1.4 from the A/R +100 USD split
```

and the A/R +100 USD split will update the basis balance to 100-100=0 USD.

We need to provide some CLI so that users can list every cost basis split with its cost and basis balance.

Say we have two income

```
A/R +100 USD            ; guid: a
Income -140 CAD         ; cost 1.4

A/R +200 USD            ; guid: b
Income -280 CAD         ; cost 1.4
```

then users should see 2 split (a, 1.4 CAD/USD, 100 USD) and (b, 1.4 CAD/USD, 200 USD)

If users want to sell 200 USD, they can either choose only b, or choose a 100 USD and b 100 USD, or choose a 50 USD and b 150 USD

## Fix

1. **Reject posting a USD invoice or bill to a CAD A/R or A/P**, naming both currencies and the account.
2. **Book the income in CAD at the invoice-date rate**, by attaching that rate to the invoice before posting, and refuse when no rate covers the date instead of letting the engine abort silently.
3. **`--fx-rates` takes dated rates**, keeping the flat `USD: 1.36` form the reporting commands already accept, so one file serves every command. Rates are exact fractions; a date earlier than every quote is an error, not an extrapolation.
4. **`settled_amount:` on a payment** — what the bank statement shows — with `share_price:` accepted as the same fact stated as a rate, passed to `gncOwnerApplyPaymentSecs`, so a USD invoice settled into a CAD bank credits what the bank actually gave — and, since settling at another rate than the record was booked at realizes a gain or loss, the payment values what it settles at that cost basis and the block places the difference with an ordinary split line of its own — `Income:FX Gain $residual$ CAD` — drawing the basis down to nothing. The engine alone values the A/R side at the settlement rate, so the entry balances and the difference vanishes.
5. **`$residual$`** so the FX gain/loss split takes what the others leave over.
6. **A KVP slot on the USD split holding its basis balance**, updated as sales pick that cost basis. A sale carries, on its own USD split, the guid of the split whose cost basis it picks, so a sale measured against two cost bases has two USD splits, and picking more than a basis's balance is refused on import. The cost is `share_price`, which the split already carries — nothing is stored for it.
7. **A CLI listing every split guid with its cost and basis balance.**
7a. **Only currency the book actually holds may be sold**: an unpaid invoice's A/R split is refused as a cost basis unless the sale carries `cost_basis_force: true`, deleting a sale returns what it took, and a record whose cost basis something measures against cannot be unposted until those transactions are removed — unposting destroys the split the basis lives on, and re-posting would silently restore the whole amount.
8. **`--include-business-objects` implies `--all-accounts`**, so an export is always re-importable.

## Verification

Every case runs against a real GnuCash book in Docker, on all supported distributions:

- a CAD invoice and bill still post, pay, and round-trip unchanged
- posting a USD record to a CAD A/R or A/P is refused
- income lands in CAD at the invoice-date rate, and no rate is an error
- a payment converts at the rate given; the rate is required when the currencies differ and rejected when they match
- settling at a rate other than the one the record was booked at realizes the difference, on the invoice side and the bill side, and the cost basis it settles falls to nothing; settling in the record's own currency realizes nothing and establishes no second basis
- `$residual$` books the gain, and two residuals or an already-balanced transaction are errors
- every split guid reads back from a book with its cost and basis balance — from an invoice, a bill, a borrowing and a purchase
- two splits at the same cost stay two, each with its own basis balance
- a sale picks the cost basis of one split, or spreads across several, in whatever amounts the user gives
- picking more than a split has available is refused on import
- an uncollected receivable cannot be sold against unless forced; deleting a sale restores the basis; unposting a record whose basis is in use is refused, on both the invoice and the bill side
- the balance sheet, income statement and account balances hold up with USD and CAD in one book

## A payment block carries the realized difference and nothing else

**Breaking**: a payment block that previously carried an ordinary split line beside `$residual$` — a wire fee, most likely — is now refused, and `$residual$` must post to an income or expense account. Files written against the earlier shape stop importing, with a message naming the line and what to do with it.

The realized difference is the one figure in a settlement that nobody moved: it is what the rate did, and no bank statement has a line for it. Everything else in a payment moved money, with its own date and counterparty, and is imported as its own transaction like anything else a bank brings in.

What made this a correctness question rather than a matter of taste is the rate. The settlement's rate came off the bank line, which is net of anything taken out of it, so a 100.00 USD invoice overpaid with 200.00 USD that produced 274.00 CAD — 2.00 kept as a fee, 272.00 credited — priced the currency at 272/200 = 1.36. The entry balanced, because `$residual$` absorbs whatever is left, so the only trace was a prepayment carrying a cost basis of 1.36 CAD/USD and a loss reported as 2.00 rather than 3.00: a dollar of bank charge had become the cost of 100.00 USD the book still held, and every later sale of it would be measured against that. With nothing but the residual in the block, the bank line *is* the whole conversion and the question cannot arise.

Nothing in the refusal decides what a line is. An account is not a charge because of its name, and this tool never reads one that way; the only test is whether the line is `$residual$`. The residual's own account is checked by *type* — income or expense — because a realized difference is a gain or a loss, and sent to an asset it balances the entry while never reaching the income statement.

Both of those, and the currency check beside them, are made before the settlement lowers the cost basis it releases. Everything they need is in the block and the account tree, so nothing has to be undone to refuse them.

That matters because the payment path has no give-back of its own. The transaction path pairs its drawdown with `give_back_to_cost_bases`; here what a late refusal would have to give back is not one drawdown but everything the invoice has already done, so the answer is to check first and, where a refusal can only come later — a realized difference with no split to take it, say — to abandon the import entirely. Measured on a book holding 200.00 USD across two bases: the refusal leaves both bases at their full balances, and an ordinary transaction sitting in the same file does not land either. The file lands in full or not at all, which is the opposite of the transaction path, where a refused transaction is dropped and the rest of the file is imported.

The wider rule this serves: a fee is an expense, not a worse rate, which is what `cost_of` has always done for a purchase stated in CAD — 40.00 USD bought for 54.00 CAD with a 2.70 fee costs 1.35, not 1.4175, because each split there is valued in CAD already and its own value over its own amount is its cost. The one shape where a fee can still move a cost is a transaction stated in the *foreign* currency, where the rate must come from the CAD splits and is pooled across all of them: a CAD line converted at another rate moves the aggregate, as `fx_two_base_splits_at_different_rates.txt` shows at 25/18. That is the aggregate of what the transaction did, and the alternative — reading whichever CAD split comes first — makes the cost depend on split order.

## A stored cost outranked the ledger

Found while reviewing this work, traced on a real book, and fixed here.

`cost_basis_cost` was added for the one shape whose transaction states no cost at all — every split in one foreign currency, so no base-currency figure to divide (a USD invoice overpaid from a USD bank). Everywhere else the cost is read from the transaction and nothing is stored, because a stored copy is a second answer that can disagree with the first.

`cost_of` consulted the copy **first**, so wherever both existed the copy won. A file could then state a cost that the transaction it sits in contradicts, and the contradiction was believed.

The trace, on a 100.00 USD purchase that cost 135.00 CAD (`tests/fixtures/stated_cost_contradicting_the_transaction.txt`), reading the split before and after the KVP is written:

```
amount           = 100
value            = 135
tx currency      = CAD
value/amount     = 27/20                     <- 1.35, the money that moved
base per unit    = 1
stated_cost_of   = None
cost_of          = 27/20                     <- correct

--- after cost_basis_cost: "9.99 CAD/USD" is written on the split ---
stated_cost_of   = 999/100
cost_of          = 999/100                   <- the copy replaced the ledger
value/amount     = 27/20                     <- unchanged, and still what happened
```

and the book, saved and read back:

```
$ gnucash-plaintext fx-balances book.gnucash
2026-01-10   038feb33…   Assets:Bank:USD   9.99 CAD/USD   100.00 USD
```

The derivation was never wrong. `cost_of` returned before reaching it.

What followed from that: `fx-balances` reports the copy; `_require_stated_cost` makes every later sale value what it sells at the copy, so the realized gain is measured from a cost no CAD in the book ever equalled; and correcting the transaction cannot dislodge it, because the correction changes only the figures the copy is hiding.

**Fix.** The transaction is consulted first and the stored cost is the fallback, which is what the docstring always claimed and what `record_borrowed_basis` already assumed when deciding not to write one. A derived cost of zero counts as no answer — a cross-currency posting GnuCash wrote with amount 0 is exactly that shape — so it falls back rather than reporting a basis that cost nothing. Stating a cost on a split the transaction already prices is refused at import as well, on both the create and the update path: the disagreement is now impossible to act on, and pointless to write.

**And a way to see it.** The same fix that stopped the copy from being believed also stopped it from being visible, so `fx-balances --verify-costs` re-checks every basis against the ledger it is derived from and prints the whole computation behind anything that disagrees: both guids, the amount and value, each factor the derivation multiplied, and both answers with the one used. It runs the entire book and exits 1 at the end rather than stopping at the first, and a basis whose figures cannot be read is reported with its traceback instead of ending the pass. Only factors the computation actually used are printed: a CAD transaction has one, and reporting a second at 1 would be stating arithmetic that never happened.

Both figures a file can state about a basis — `cost_basis_cost:` and `cost_basis_balance:` — are checked before they land, on the create path and the update path alike. The balance is the one that can conjure currency: nothing downstream questions it, so 150.00 stated on a split that acquired 100.00 leaves 50.00 sellable that never arrived, and the gain on selling it is measured against a cost that was really paid. It must be on a split that holds foreign currency at all — the wrong line of a purchase's two takes the balance silently and leaves the real basis to open at its full amount — and then a number, not negative, no finer than the unit its own account is held to, and no more than that split brought in. The unparseable case was the quiet one: `60,00` for `60.00` left the split marked as having stated a balance — so the sale below it in the same file was skipped as already accounted for — while the basis, having nothing readable, opened at its full amount. Forty sold USD came back with no error, and the figure afterwards parses, so nothing later finds it odd.

Two things are checked in a book already written: that no basis balance is above what its basis brought in or below zero, and that a stored cost parses and agrees with the transaction. Both are exact comparisons against figures the ledger already holds — the split's own amount, zero, and the transaction's own cost — with no tolerance or inferred window in either.

Two more were written and removed, and what they have in common is worth recording, because each looked reasonable and each reported correct books as broken.

The first compared a split's `share_price` against its value. GnuCash stores no rate — `xaccSplitGetSharePrice` divides value by amount on demand, measured by setting a rate of 1.405 on 45.00 USD (value 63.23, rate reads back 6323/4500) and then setting the value to 63.00 (rate reads back 6300/4500) — so the comparison was one number against itself and could not fail. It was documented in three places as one of the things checked, which made a clean run read as an assurance nothing had tested.

The second asked whether a transaction's base-currency splits agree about its rate. **Rates run forward only.** The file states 1.405, the amount is multiplied by it, and the result is rounded to the unit the account is held to; the effective rate the ledger then carries is 6323/4500, which is the rounding working rather than a discrepancy. Reading that figure back to ask which rate produced it has no answer — many rates give 63.23, and the stated one is not among the things a book keeps. Three criteria were tried in that backwards direction and every one of them reported correct books:

- the splits' ratios against each other, which flags every taxed foreign invoice: 33.33 USD at 10% tax, posted at 1.4, books 46.66 CAD over 33.33 and 4.66 over 3.33, ratios of 1.40006 and 1.39940;
- each split against the pooled rate, which flags a bill of 1.819 CAD for 1.30 USD beside 5.00 for 3.57 — a rate of 1.3992 produces both exactly, while the pool of 6.819 over 4.87 implies 1.820 for the first;
- the windows the rounding leaves, which was the same inference wearing an interval.

What survives from that work is the part that was never a test: the rate a cost is derived from is every base-currency split pooled, so it does not depend on which split is read first — a book listing its tax line before its income line had priced the basis at 1.3994 — and figures are printed at the unit their account is held to, so fuel kept to thousandths shows 1.819 rather than a 1.82 the ledger does not contain.

## What settled an invoice is recorded, not inferred

`auto_apply_credit: true` is a request — apply whatever credit this owner has — and what a book holds afterwards is an outcome: this much, out of that split. The export records the outcome, in a payment block carrying `from_credit: true`, the amount taken, the split it came from and `credit_dated:` for the day that currency arrived. Nothing a person writes changes; the flag is still the whole of what asks for a credit to be spent.

Which of an invoice's payments came out of credit cannot be worked out afterwards, and three readings were tried before the book was made to record it. Asking whether the transaction still touches a leftover credit lot answers for every invoice that transaction settles at once — so the invoice a bank transfer paid claimed a credit had paid it — and says no for a credit consumed to the last cent. Asking whether the payment predates the invoice's posting is right everywhere except the day a deposit is taken and an invoice raised against it, where no figure in the book separates the two. So the import writes `applied_from_credit` on the splits it moves, and the export reads that. A book from before the key existed keeps its old reading, faults and all, rather than being unposted and rebuilt.

Dividing a credit is done here rather than by the engine. `AutoApplyPayments` chooses among the owner's credits in its own order and divides differently by version — 5.10 carves the split, 4.13 and 3.8 move it whole — so a block naming one credit could have another carved, and the remainder could land where nothing can see it. What the file names is what is spent.

A credit bigger than the invoice it settles is an overpayment by another name, and takes the path an overpaying bank transfer has taken since Q-015: the invoice is settled with exactly what it owes, and what is left becomes the owner's credit in a lot of its own. One function does it for both — `settle_from_one_split` — and the caller's whole contribution is which split covers the invoice. A bank transfer's is found as the side that is not the bank; a `from_credit:` block gives its own by guid. The split the caller hands over is the one that settles the invoice, so a file that divided a credit carries the guid of the split that settled it, and reading that file again is no change at all.

Two things differ, and only two. Where the money came from decides what the leftover credit costs: a bank transfer states no base-currency figure, so the record's own carried cost prices it; a credit already carried a cost, and what is left of its balance moves onto what is left of it — the smaller of the balance and the residue, since a credit half sold has less to give than its size suggests. And a credit in no lot cannot be divided at all, because parking the residue means naming an owner for it, which only a lot records.
